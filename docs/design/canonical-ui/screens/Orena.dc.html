Present perfect vs past simple
Grammar · B1–B2 · from your Writing finding
✓ Past simple→Present perfect vs past simple→Present perfect continuous
Past simple closes an event in the past. Present perfect connects a past event or state to now.
Pattern
Subject+have / has+past participle
She has lived here for eleven years.
We have never figured it out.
Timeline
movednowhave had this routine (still true)
Use present perfect when…
  • a state started in the past and continues: I've lived here for three years
  • the time is unfinished or unstated: I've grown to like it
  • the result matters now: I've figured out where to stand
Use past simple when…
  • the event is finished at a known time: I moved here in 2023
  • the moment is closed: the tram was packed this morning
  • a sequence of finished actions: I grabbed a croissant and left
Common mistake · from your draft
Since I moved to this city, I have this routine.
Since I moved to this city, I have had this routine.
“Since + past simple” sets the starting point; the main clause needs present perfect to reach “now”.
