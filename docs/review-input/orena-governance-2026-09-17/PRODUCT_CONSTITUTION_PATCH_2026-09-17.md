## Orena product direction

Orena is not primarily a toolbox of language-learning features. It is a language-learning product in which learners encounter meaningful content, situations, people, stories, ideas and media, and use language to understand and participate in them.

The product direction is:

> **Clarity first. Curiosity next. Depth over time.**

> **Simple front door. Deep world behind it.**

The "world" concept is an internal product/design principle. It must NOT become learner-facing lore, fantasy terminology, or a metaphor users must understand before they can learn.

### Product hierarchy

1. A new learner must always have an obvious way to start.
2. Guided learning and open exploration must coexist.
3. Content is the primary carrier of curiosity and richness.
4. Skills such as reading, listening, speaking, writing, vocabulary and grammar remain explicit capabilities and shortcuts, but they do not need to be the only way content is organized.
5. AI stays backstage and supports the learning experience rather than becoming the product's visible protagonist.
6. Depth should reveal itself progressively as the learner gains history, saved language, interests and progress.

### Beginner-safe front door

A learner who has never learned a language and never used a language-learning app must be able to:

- choose a language;
- state or infer a starting level;
- see one obvious primary action;
- begin a short first learning experience without understanding Orena's internal architecture.

Orena must never require users to learn how Orena works before they can start learning a language.

### Guided path + Explore

The product must support both:

**Guided path** — for learners who want to be led and do not know what to learn next.

**Explore** — for learners who want to browse books, stories, videos, audio, topics and other content.

These are two entry modes into the same learning/content system, not separate products.

### Library and Discover

> **Library stores the world. Discover reveals the world. Learning tools help the learner interact with it.**

Library may be practical, searchable and filterable.

Discover should reveal meaningful selections from the same content system rather than duplicate content into isolated skill silos.

A single content object should be able to support multiple learning actions where appropriate: reading, listening, transcript interaction, vocabulary, pronunciation, grammar/pattern explanation, shadowing, speaking, writing and recall.

### Language of the interface

Clear learner-facing terms are preferred over clever metaphors.

Labels such as Reading, Listening, Speaking, Writing, Vocabulary, Library and Practice are valid when they improve comprehension.

Personality should come primarily from content, artwork, composition, icons, color, motion and interaction — not from making navigation harder to understand.

### Learner-facing philosophy rule

> **Never explain the Orena philosophy to the learner when the interface can let them experience it.**

Avoid learner-facing product philosophy copy, lore and slogans that do not help the current task.
